## What is new in the chapter

The dot product v·w = v₁w₁ + … + v_mw_m, a scalar; its commutativity, scalar and distributive properties; v·v = |v|² and, by the Law of Cosines, v·w = |v||w| cos θ, so the dot product measures lengths and angles; orthogonal vectors, v·w = 0, perpendicular vectors and the zero vector; the similarity of vectors as the angle between them (Wikipedia articles as word-count vectors), time series, demeaning and the correlation as the cosine of the angle between demeaned series; the distance between points as |v − w|, clustering, the centroid, the k-means algorithm, the objective as the average squared distance to the centroid, the dependence on the initial choice, and the elbow plot for choosing k (6.1). The orthogonal complement W⊥ of a subspace, itself a subspace, found as the solution set of the linear system of dot-product equations; the transpose Aᵀ, whose rows are the columns of A; Aᵀx lists the dot products of the columns of A with x; Col(A)⊥ = Nul(Aᵀ); (A + B)ᵀ = Aᵀ + Bᵀ, (sA)ᵀ = sAᵀ, (Aᵀ)ᵀ = A, det(C) = det(Cᵀ), (AB)ᵀ = BᵀAᵀ, (Cᵀ)⁻¹ = (C⁻¹)ᵀ; rank(A) = rank(Aᵀ), stated and deferred to 7.4; dim W + dim W⊥ = m; in the cards and exercises: symmetric matrices, xᵀy, the row space, the Pythagorean theorem for orthogonal vectors, the eigenvectors of a symmetric matrix being orthogonal (6.2). Orthogonal sets, the weights cᵢ = (b·wᵢ)/(wᵢ·wᵢ) of a linear combination of one, their linear independence and so orthogonal bases; unit vectors and u = (1/|w|) w; orthonormal sets and bases, QᵀQ = Iₙ; the orthogonal projection b̂ of b onto W as the closest vector, characterized by b − b̂ orthogonal to W (the Pythagorean argument); the projection onto a line, the projection formula, the orthonormal form b̂ = Σ(b·uᵢ)uᵢ = QQᵀb and the projection matrix P = QQᵀ with Col(P) = W; b = b̂ + b⊥; QQᵀ = I only when Q is square; orthogonal matrices, Q⁻¹ = Qᵀ; in the exercises, P = wwᵀ/|w|², |Qx| = |x|, det Q = ±1, every 2 × 2 orthogonal matrix a rotation or a reflection (6.3). Gram–Schmidt orthogonalization, each wⱼ being vⱼ minus its projection onto the span of the earlier ones, the spans preserved, normalization to an orthonormal basis, failure on a dependent set; the QR factorization A = QR of a matrix with linearly independent columns, Q with orthonormal columns and R upper triangular, found by writing each column of A in the orthonormal basis; Sage's own `QR` differs, so the author supplies `QR(A)` (6.4). Least-squares: when Ax = b is inconsistent, solve Ax = b̂; the line b + mx = y through data points as a system with A = [1 xᵢ], the sum of squares |b − Ax|² of the vertical distances, the line of best fit; the least-squares approximate solution x̂, unique when the columns of A are independent; the normal equation AᵀAx̂ = Aᵀb; linear regression (the cricket chirps, the body fat index from five measurements) and predictions; demeaning, the coefficient of determination R² = 1 − |b − Ax̂|²/|b̃|² between 0 and 1; the QR route x̂ = R⁻¹Qᵀb, numerically more reliable than the normal equation; polynomial regression, the Vandermonde matrix, noise and overfitting in the Arctic sea ice fit; in the exercises, Kepler's law by a log–log fit, 1057 houses, the uniqueness of the normal equation's solution, and R² as the fraction of variance explained (6.5).
