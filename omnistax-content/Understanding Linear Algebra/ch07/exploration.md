## What is new in the chapter

Orthogonal diagonalization A = QDQᵀ when there is an orthonormal basis of eigenvectors; symmetric matrices A = Aᵀ; the Spectral Theorem (orthogonally diagonalizable if and only if symmetric) and its consequences (real eigenvalues, a basis of eigenvectors, orthogonal eigenvectors for distinct eigenvalues); v·(Aw) = (Aᵀv)·w; the factorization Ax = Q(D(Qᵀx)) as rotate, stretch, rotate back; total variance as the mean squared distance of the demeaned points from the origin, variance in a direction, the additivity of variance over an orthonormal basis (by the Pythagorean theorem), the covariance matrix C = (1/N)AAᵀ, symmetric, and V_u = u·(Cu) (7.1). Quadratic forms q_A(x) = x·(Ax) and their expression ax₁² + 2bx₁x₂ + cx₂², q_A(sx) = s²q_A(x), q_A(x) = λ|x|² on an eigenvector; the maximum and minimum of q_A on unit vectors as the largest and smallest eigenvalues, in the directions of their eigenvectors; the Principal Axes Theorem; positive definite, semidefinite, negative definite and indefinite matrices read from eigenvalues; the quadratic approximation near a critical point, the Hessian and the Second Derivative Test by eigenvalues, with the calculus texts' det(H) version (7.2). Principal components, the eigenvectors of C in decreasing order of eigenvalue; the fraction of variance retained by W_n, which no other n-dimensional subspace beats; dimensional reduction; the coordinates Qᵀx of the projected data; the UK diet, the iris and the penguin data (7.3). The length l_A(x) = |Ax|, the Gram matrix G = AᵀA with l_A = √q_G; singular values as the square roots of G's eigenvalues, right singular vectors as its eigenvectors, left singular vectors from Avᵢ = σᵢuᵢ, orthonormal; the singular value decomposition A = UΣVᵀ of every m × n matrix; Aᵀ = VΣᵀUᵀ; rank as the number of nonzero singular values; orthonormal bases of the four fundamental subspaces Col(A), Nul(Aᵀ), Col(Aᵀ) = Row(A), Nul(A); rank(A) = rank(Aᵀ), which 6.2 deferred to here; the reduced SVD A = U_rΣ_rV_rᵀ (7.4).
