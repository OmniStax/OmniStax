# Chapter 7 colour

Refines the book's `COLOR.md`; no new hue. Prepared 2026-11-02 with `config.md`.

## By root rule 7's four ways

1. Facts: the grayscale images of 7.5 (the 316 × 310 photograph of `utah-gray.png`, the 25 × 15 letter O and its noisy copy) are one scalar, the pixel value, on one gray scale with its legend (`$BOOK/COLOR.md`, facts: "a greyscale block is one scalar on one scale"); the figure row lists `"spectrum"` only if the plan draws a colour ramp, else the grays it paints. The iris photograph (7.3.4) and the penguin artwork (7.3.5) are kept images, not drawn, and carry their own colours. Nothing else is a fact: the author's red and blue in the singular-values interactive and the Supreme Court's red (−1) and white (+1) squares of `display_matrix` are the author's choices, not facts; a Supreme Court Sim, if built, draws +1 and −1 on one diverging scale with its legend, as a false-colour map of one scalar (root rule 7.1).
2. Conventions: none.
3. Referents: the three iris species of 7.3's PCA Sim (`setosa`, `versicolor`, `virginica`; `$BOOK/COLOR.md` names "the species of 7.3's iris data") and the three penguin species of the same Sim's other dataset (`adelie`, `chinstrap`, `gentoo`), which the text and the cards name apart; the four nations of the UK diet (`england`, `northern-ireland`, `scotland`, `wales`), if 7.3 builds that Sim, named apart in Activity 7.3.4's card and the text after it ("Northern Ireland lies far to the right"); in 7.5's Supreme Court Sim, if built, the justices are labels along an axis, not referents (nine would crowd the group, and the text names only O'Connor and Kennedy apart, so the plan may make those two referents). No other referent: the eigenvectors v₁, v₂ of 7.1 and the singular vectors v₁, v₂, u₁, u₂ of 7.4 are told apart by their labels and by decoration (the first solid, the second dashed), not by referent colours, since the subscript is the singular value's index and colouring it would compete with `eigenvalue` on σ₁, σ₂ in the same readout; the three data points of Activity 7.1.4 and Example 7.2.7 are `point`, labelled d̃₁, d̃₂, d̃₃.
4. Categories: `vector` for every arrow and its bold letter (v, w, x, u, y, b, the eigenvectors v₁, v₂, vᵢ, the orthonormal u₁, u₂, uⱼ, the principal components u₁, u₂, …, the right and left singular vectors vᵢ, uᵢ, x̂, b̂, m, the input x of a quadratic form, Ax in Figure 7.4.1 and its image σᵢuᵢ), the components in a readout in ink; `matrix` for A, Aᵀ, B, D, P, P⁻¹, Q, Qᵀ, C, G, H, U, Σ, V, Vᵀ, U_r, Σ_r, V_r, U_k, Σ_k, V_k, Σ⁽ᵏ⁾, Γ, Γ_k, A⁺, A_k, A₁ … A₄ wherever a figure or readout draws one as a whole, and for the image of the unit grid, the unit square and the unit circle under a matrix (the transformed grid of 7.1.2, 7.1.3 and 7.1.8, the ellipse of 7.4.1, drawn in `matrix`'s hue, the original grid and circle in ink, as `$BOOK/COLOR.md` says); `region` for the subspaces W, W₁, W₂, W₃, W_n (the plane of 7.3.1 and the principal plane), Col, Nul, Row, the line through the origin along which the variance is measured in 7.1's Sim and in 7.2.8 + 7.2.9 (a thin long line, the span of u), and the eigenspaces of the exercises; `eigenvalue` for λ, λᵢ, the singular values σ, σᵢ (as numbers in the readout and as the bar height |Ax| = σ in Figure 7.4.1, whose marks at σ₁ and σ₂ wear it), and the variances V, V_u, V_{u₁}, V_x, V_y, V_W (as numbers in the readout and as the bars or lengths a figure draws for them); `point` for the data points dⱼ, the centroid d̄ (hollow, a variant) and the demeaned points d̃ⱼ, the projections of the points onto the line (a variant, smaller or hollow), the 100 points of Activity 7.1.6, the cloud of 7.3.1 and the irises and penguins of the PCA Sim before their species colour them (an unnamed point keeps `point`; a point of a species wears its referent). `curve` is not used: the level curves of q_A in 7.2 are contours of one scalar, drawn in ink (or muted), not a type; q_A(u) along the unit circle is drawn as a graph in ink with its extreme values marked in `eigenvalue`. The unit circle, axes, grid, the projection segments from a point to the line and the right-angle marks are ink.
