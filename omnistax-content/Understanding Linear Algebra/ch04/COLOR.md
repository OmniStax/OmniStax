# Chapter 4 colour

Refines the book's `COLOR.md`; no new hue. Prepared 2026-11-02 with `config.md`.

## By root rule 7's four ways

1. Facts: none. No figure of the chapter draws a colour that means anything by its nature; the board game photograph is not served.
2. Conventions: none.
3. Referents: the subscripted eigenvectors of one figure that the text names apart, the species, locations, parties and pages whose quantities a figure draws one bar or one curve each (below). An unsubscripted pair such as v and w (Example 4.1.2) is not a referent pair.
4. Categories: `vector` for every arrow and its bold letter (v, w, x, x_k, x₀, the eigenvectors v₁, v₂, v₃, v_j, the columns of P, the probability vectors, q, e₁, e₂) and for the coordinate vector {x_k}_𝓑; `matrix` for A, B, C, C′, D, Dᵏ, E, I, P, P⁻¹, P′, Aᵏ, A⁻¹, G, G′, Hₙ and S wherever a figure or a readout draws one as a whole, and for the image of anything under a matrix: Av beside v (4.1.3), the image of the eigenbasis unit square under A and of the standard square under D (4.1.6 + 4.1.7, 4.3.1), the grid carried by P⁻¹, D and P in 4.3's Sim; `region` for the eigenspaces E_λ and E₁ and for Nul(A − λI), and for the eigenvector lines of a phase portrait where the text names them as the eigenspaces E_{λ₁}, E_{λ₂} the trajectories converge to (drawn thin and long, the arrows v₁, v₂ on them in `vector`); `eigenvalue` for λ, λ₁, λ₂, λ₃, λ_j in every readout, matched to its line or axis in the figure, and for a complex eigenvalue a + bi as a whole; `point` for the state x_k at one time, the dots on a trajectory and the riding point under the transport, and for the iterates Aᵏx of Exercises 4.3.9–4.3.10's answers; `curve` for a trajectory, the parametric curve through the iterates. The standard grid and the eigenbasis grid of linear combinations are ink, as in Chapters 2 and 3; the unit square's outline is ink. A Markov chain drawn as bars draws the components of x_k, scalars in ink, each bar in its referent's colour (rule 7.3). Symbols: `\kvecv`, `\kvecw`, `\kvecx`, `\kvecxk`, `\kvecxzero`, `\kvecvone` … `\kvecvthree`, `\kvecvi`, `\kvecq`, `\kveceone`, `\kvecetwo`, `\kvecxB` wear `vector`; `\kA`, `\kB`, `\kC`, `\kCprime`, `\kD`, `\kDk`, `\kE`, `\kI`, `\kP`, `\kPinv`, `\kPprime`, `\kAk`, `\kAinv`, `\kGprime`, `\kHn`, `\kS` (4.5's exercises) wear `matrix`; `\kElambda`, `\kEone`, `\kNul` wear `region`; `\klambda`, `\klambdaone`, `\klambdatwo`, `\klambdathree`, `\klambdaj` wear `eigenvalue`. The Google matrix G has the macro `\kG` (added by the orchestrator before 4.5 was built; its key `G` is Chapter 3's green value, and the 4.5 row carries the `google-matrix` meaning with `redefines`), and 4.5 writes it through the macro.

Untyped, in ink: a multiplicity m, m_j; the entries a, b, c, d of a 2 × 2 matrix (4.1.3's sliders) and the parts a, b of λ = a + bi; r and θ; the imaginary unit i; the weights c₁, c₂; the index k, the size n, the count p of distinct eigenvalues and the parameter p of Exercises 4.4.2–4.4.3; the counts P_k, Q_k, R_k, S_k, J_k, Y_k, A_k and the percentages of voters; a probability; a PageRank x_j; α; the letters P, Q, R, S for locations, species and parties; a basis 𝓑; ℝⁿ; det, dim, rank.
